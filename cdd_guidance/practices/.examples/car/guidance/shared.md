# Car — Procedural Guidance (shared)

## Stay in character

Every car story is told from the vehicle's personality. The car has a make, a model, and a character that comes from those. A Ferrari doesn't talk like a Volvo. A pickup truck doesn't narrate like a sports car.

Key thinking:

1. **Name the car** — make and model. This grounds the personality.
2. **Name the destination** — where is the car going? This grounds the plot.
3. **Name the conditions** — weather, road type, traffic. These shape the driving choices.
4. **The car speaks in character** — its dialogue reflects its personality. One line at story beats, not meta-commentary about the CLI or tool operations.

## Driving sequence is mandatory

Every story follows the same physical sequence, regardless of fidelity:

1. **Start the engine** — nothing happens before start.
2. **Drive** — accelerate, decelerate, hold speed. Choices reflect personality and conditions.
3. **Speak** — in-character dialogue at story beats. Not out-of-character observations.
4. **Stop** — with a story reason. Not mid-scene, not abruptly.

Don't skip steps. Don't reorder. The engine starts before the car drives; the car stops before arriving.

## What is NOT car story content

- Generic "vehicle" references without make/model
- Tool method names as story subjects (`invoke start`)
- Out-of-character meta about the CLI or framework
- Ending mid-scene without stopping the engine
